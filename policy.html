JIM WEISSER / POLICY

Policy & public work

My first policy work was about encryption in Japan in 1996. I've cared about technology policy ever since.

From encryption to the internet economy

In 1996, I worked with US companies in Japan on rules that would let them use and sell encryption-related products here. At the American Chamber of Commerce in Japan, I later started the E-Business Committee, ran the ICT Committee, and now chair the Digital Transformation Committee.

I was heavily involved in the ACCJ's Internet Economy Task Force and in preparing and releasing its 2009 internet economy white paper. We argued that internet policy could benefit both Japan and the United States, rather than becoming another zero-sum trade dispute.

I was at the US Embassy when we agreed to push for a dialogue on that basis, working with Eriko Asai, Jim Foster of Microsoft and others. The formal government dialogue began in 2010. ACCJ and Keidanren continued the business-side work, including a joint statement in 2012 and another in 2014.

ACCJ leadership

I served as an ACCJ governor from 2008 to 2010 and as a vice president from 2011 to 2014. I was on the board when the chamber received the US Department of Commerce's Peace Through Commerce award for 2012, announced in 2013.

Working in a volunteer organization taught me something I needed as a manager: people have to want to work with you. A title alone does not get the work done.
