Nareji

Current work · Agentic Agreement Management

Nareji uses AI to identify key dates, parties and obligations in agreements, so companies can manage what they signed rather than just store the document. It is designed to work across signing platforms, email attachments and scanned paper.

I first ran into this problem at my third company, PBXL: agreements, contracts and quotes were signed and stored, then received little attention as the business moved on.

We have two goals: give larger companies current visibility into the agreements and obligations of their subsidiaries, and help smaller businesses manage their commitments proactively so they don't miss deadlines.

SignTime

Founder and CEO, 2020–2026 · CTO / head of product

In 2020, I founded SignTime, a web-based e-signature service for sending contracts by email or SMS and getting them signed. We develop in short cycles, work through partners, and support API connections to other systems. My current work is as CTO and head of product; the chairmanship is planned for 2027.

Customers include Sakana AI, Qsol in the Kyuden group, and Tokai. They are different businesses with different uses for electronic signatures.

PBXL, BroadSoft and Cisco

PBXL founded 2006 · acquired by BroadSoft in 2015 · BroadSoft acquired by Cisco in 2018

PBXL provided cloud-based business telephony. We were early to the market, and I underestimated both how long adoption would take and how much distribution mattered. In 2013, we announced a service on the BroadSoft platform that brought voice, video, text and fax together.

When I went to raise growth capital in 2014, four separate buyers approached us. I took that as a sign to sell. BroadSoft acquired PBXL in 2015 and used the team as the basis for its BroadCloud expansion in Japan. We closed deals with SoftBank and KDDI and made progress with NTT Communications. After the acquisition, NIFTY and later BIGLOBE were announced as BroadCloud customers.

Cisco acquired BroadSoft in 2018. I wanted to get back to startups, but I also wanted the people who had come with me to have good opportunities inside Cisco. I stayed to help with that transition and left in 2019.

Weisser Consulting / Weisser Ideas

Early 2000s · Consulting

After Enron, I started Weisser Consulting. The work included telecommunications expense management, data centers, security and project management. I often found errors in metered billing or services customers were paying for but not using. Much of the business came through personal outreach, and contingency sales can be hard sometimes when the buyer is trying to avoid uncertainty.

The business did okay, but revenue depended heavily on what I could deliver myself. Seeing the same communications problems at different customers eventually led me to PBXL.
