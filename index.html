TOKYO / SOFTWARE / BUSINESS

Jim Weisser

I build software businesses in Japan. I’m also doing development again for the first time in 30 years.

From chemical engineering to software

I studied chemical engineering at Rice University and came to Japan in 1993. What was supposed to be a year or two teaching English became a career in internet infrastructure, telecommunications and software.

I’m working on Nareji, which uses AI to identify obligations in signed agreements. I’m also the co-founder and CEO of SignTime. Before that, I co-founded PBXL, which BroadSoft acquired in 2015. BroadSoft later became part of Cisco. There have been other companies along the way, including two that didn’t work out.

The work I like

I particularly enjoy helping companies succeed in industries that aren’t very digital. Japanese construction and food businesses are good examples. I think of it as the inverse Y Combinator universe: customers have substantial businesses, but software is often a small part of how they operate.

Getting the technology to work is part of the job. Getting people to buy it, use it and keep using it has usually taken more work than I expected.
